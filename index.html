Algorithm design

Distance-to-Empty algorithm

Most scooters multiply battery percentage by a fixed number, so the estimate falls apart on a hill or after a night parked. The DTE algorithm blends recent riding history with instant throttle power, corrects for low-battery voltage sag, temperature and parking drain, then smooths the readout so it doesn't jump around. It runs on Raspberry Pi / ESP32-class hardware using physics formulas rather than a heavy ML model.

Failure analysis

Why were the Hall sensors failing?

Hall sensors inside a hub motor kept failing in the field, and nobody knew what the inside of a running motor actually looked like. I built a small ESP32 rig with temperature and humidity sensors mounted inside the motor and logged it through real riding sessions.

The answer wasn't what we expected: the risky moment isn't while riding. It's right after the motor stops, when moisture condenses near the Hall sensor board.

Protocol tooling

Talking to the battery over RS-485

I wrote a desktop tool in Python and PyQt5 that talks to the battery management system over RS-485, checks every frame with CRC-16-Modbus and shows the battery's live data: charge, health, voltage, current, temperatures and fault flags.

I then used it to test a new BMS against a reference unit and documented the firmware bugs it turned up, with suggested fixes.

Reverse engineering

Listening in on a production scooter's CAN bus

No documentation, no DBC file. I tapped CAN_H and CAN_L at the VCU, MCU and battery coupler of an Ola S1 Pro Gen 2 and captured traffic with CANTest and USBCAN-2E analyzers, across two firmware versions.

Beyond passive capture, I ran structured tests: riding scenarios, pulling a node off the bus, and swapping in a second MCU, to see how the network reacts and recovers.
